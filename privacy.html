MatchTrack — Privacy Policy

Last updated 17 October 2026

MatchTrack collects no personal information, and your work never leaves your Mac.

There are no accounts, no sign-in, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking of any kind. Your reports, players and files are never sent to us or to anyone. The one thing we receive is Apple's notice when a subscription changes, described under Subscriptions below, and it contains nothing that identifies you.

Where your data lives

Everything you load or create — GPS exports, player names, session dates, opposition, your saved reports, team branding, opponent badges and player photographs — is stored on your own Mac, in the app's folder inside your Library. None of it reaches us and none of it reaches anyone else.

Reports you export as PDF or CSV are ordinary files saved to your Downloads folder. Those copies are yours; where they go afterwards and who can see them is entirely under your control.

Player data

The names, physical performance figures and photographs in this app describe real people, often young athletes. They come from GPS exports and photos you supply, they are written only to your own Mac, and nothing in the app uploads, shares or analyses them anywhere else.

Because that data stays with you, so does the responsibility for it. If your club has obligations about handling player information, they apply to the files on your Mac and to any report you choose to share.

Network use

The app runs a small web server on your Mac in order to display its own interface. It listens only on 127.0.0.1 — the loopback address, which is your Mac talking to itself. It is not reachable from your network or from the internet, and no data passes over it that is not already on your machine.

Apart from that, the app makes no network connections of its own. Apple's purchase system talks to the App Store when you subscribe or restore a purchase.

Subscriptions

The subscription is handled entirely by Apple through the App Store. Payment details never touch this app — we never see your name, your email address, your card, or your Apple Account.

Apple tells the app one thing: whether an active subscription exists. That answer is stored on your Mac so the app knows whether to unlock.

Apple also sends us a short notice when a subscription is bought, renewed, cancelled or refunded. It contains Apple's transaction number, the plan, the dates and the App Store country — no name, no email address and nothing from the app. We keep these notices for 400 days to support customers and handle refunds, and then they are deleted. They are held by Cloudflare, which runs the small service that receives them.

Apple's own handling of your purchase is covered by Apple's privacy policy, not this one.

Diagnostics

When the interface stalls noticeably, the app writes a short note about which part was slow to a small, size-capped log file on your Mac. It records timings only — no report contents, no player information. Nothing about it is transmitted; it exists so a problem can be identified from the machine it happened on.

Device identification

The app derives a one-way fingerprint from your Mac's hardware identifier so that a subscription cannot simply be copied to another machine. The fingerprint is a hash: it cannot be turned back into anything identifying, it is stored on your Mac, and it is never sent anywhere.

Children

MatchTrack is a tool for coaches and performance staff, and is not directed at children. It collects no information from anyone. Player details entered or imported by a coach stay on the Mac they were entered on.

Deleting your data

Because everything is a file on your Mac, deleting the app's folder in your Library removes it. The only thing held elsewhere is the subscription notices described above, which identify no one and are deleted after 400 days. There is no request you need to make of us.

Changes

If this policy ever changes, the updated version will be posted at this address with a new date at the top. Since the app collects nothing, any change is likely to be clarification rather than substance.
