MatchTrack — Privacy Policy

Last updated 21 October 2026

The MatchTrack app collects no personal information, and nothing you put into it is sent to us unless you send it yourself.

There are no accounts, no sign-in, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking of any kind. The app never sends your reports, players or files to us or to anyone else. They leave your Mac or iPad only when you export or share them yourself, or in your device's own backups if you use them (Time Machine, or iCloud or computer backups of an iPad). Apart from what you choose to send us when you ask for help, the one thing we receive is Apple's notice when a subscription changes, described under Subscriptions below. It carries no name and no email address, though we can match it to you if you write to us about your subscription.

This policy covers MatchTrack on Mac and on iPad. Where the two work differently, it says so.

Where your data lives

Everything you load or create — GPS exports, player names, session dates, opposition, your saved reports, team branding, opponent badges and player photographs — is stored on your own Mac or iPad, in the app's own storage. None of it reaches us, and the app sends none of it to anyone else.

  • On a Mac, it is in the app's folder inside your Library: ~/Library/Containers/com.thomasgrech.matchtrack. The Mac app runs in Apple's App Sandbox, which is why it is there and not in ~/Library/Application Support.
  • On an iPad, it is inside the app itself, in the app's own storage on the iPad.

In the same place, the app's interface keeps a working copy of your saved reports and settings, and the app keeps automatic restore points — earlier copies of your reports, GPS files and settings, but not photographs — so that a mistake can be undone. It keeps up to ten for each workspace.

Reports you export as PDF or CSV, backup files and support packages are ordinary files, made only when you ask for them. On a Mac they are saved to your Downloads folder (a PDF or CSV goes to the app's own Exports folder instead if Downloads cannot be written to). On an iPad they appear in Files › On My iPad › MatchTrack, in folders called Exports, Backups and Support, and the share sheet opens so you can AirDrop, mail, print or save them elsewhere. Those copies are yours; where they go afterwards and who can see them is entirely under your control.

A backup file is a full copy of the workspaces you choose — reports, GPS files, player photographs, badges and settings — together with the app's version and the name of the Mac or iPad it was made on. A backup made on a Mac also records where the app keeps its data on that Mac, and that folder's name includes your macOS account name. Treat it as you would the reports themselves.

Your device's own backups include the app's data, as they do for other apps: Time Machine on a Mac, and iCloud or computer backups of an iPad. On an iPad the app leaves its restore points out of those backups. MatchTrack itself keeps no copy anywhere else, and does not sync between your devices.

Files and photos you bring in

The app reads a file only when you give it one. On a Mac, that is through the standard Open window or by dragging a file into the app's window. On an iPad, it is through the Files picker, by dragging a file in from Files, or by opening a CSV or a backup file in MatchTrack from Mail, Files or AirDrop. A file opened that way is copied into the app so it can be read, and the copy is deleted once it has been read; if the app closes before then, the copy is deleted the first time the app is opened a day or more later. A file opened straight from Files is left where it is.

On an iPad, adding a player photo or a club crest offers your photo library, the camera or Files, through iPadOS's own picker. The app receives only the picture you choose, and stores it with that workspace's data.

On either device, a player photo — and usually a crest — is kept as the file you chose, so any details the file carries, such as where and when a photo was taken, stay with it, including in any backup file you make.

Player data

The names, physical performance figures and photographs in this app describe real people, often young athletes. They come from GPS exports and photos you supply, they are written only to your own Mac or iPad, and nothing in the app uploads or analyses them anywhere else. They leave the device only in a report, export or backup file that you make and send yourself, or in the device's own backups if you use them.

Because that data stays with you, so does the responsibility for it. If your club has obligations about handling player information, they apply to the data on your Mac or iPad, to that device's backups, and to any report or backup file you choose to share.

Network use

On a Mac, the app runs a small web server on your Mac in order to display its own interface. It listens only on 127.0.0.1 — the loopback address, which is your Mac talking to itself. It is not reachable from your network or from the internet, and no data passes over it that is not already on your machine.

On an iPad, there is no web server. The interface is served from inside the app, at its own internal address matchtrack://app, without any network connection.

Apart from that, neither app contacts any server of ours or any third party. Apple's purchase system talks to the App Store when the app opens, when you come back to it, from time to time while it runs to see whether a subscription has renewed or ended, and when you subscribe, restore, manage a subscription or ask for a refund. On a Mac, macOS may also look up the computer's own name on your network (see Device information). The links on the subscription screen, to Apple's terms and to this policy, open in your web browser.

Subscriptions

The subscription is handled entirely by Apple through the App Store. Payment details never touch this app, and Apple does not give us your name, your email address, your card or your Apple Account. One subscription covers MatchTrack on both Mac and iPad.

Apple tells the app whether a subscription is active, which plan it is, when it renews or ends, and whether it is in a billing grace period. That answer is stored on your Mac or iPad so the app knows whether to unlock.

Apple also sends us a short notice whenever a subscription changes — for example when it is bought, renews, is cancelled, expires, fails to renew or is refunded — on either device. It contains no name, no email address and nothing from the app. Apple's notice also includes the price paid and renewal details, which we do not keep. From each notice we keep only the kind of change, the plan, which app it is for, Apple's transaction and notice numbers, the date of the notice, the purchase, expiry and any refund dates, the App Store country, whether a free trial or other offer applied, Apple's reason code for a refund, and whether it came from Apple's test system. Apple's numbers carry no name or contact details. We keep these records for 400 days to support customers, handle refunds and watch for refund abuse, which we may report to Apple, and then they are deleted. They are held by Cloudflare, which runs the small service that receives them.

If you write to us about your subscription, we may use these records to find your purchase and help you, which connects that record to your email address. That is why the App Store lists Purchases as "Data Linked to You": it is your purchase history, used only to support you, and never for tracking or advertising. A notice about a refund or a revoked purchase may also be emailed to our support address; a copy sent that way is kept with our support email rather than deleted after 400 days.

Apple's own handling of your purchase is covered by Apple's privacy policy, not this one.

Diagnostics

When the interface stalls noticeably, the app writes a short note about which part was slow to a small, size-capped log file on your Mac or iPad. It records timings, which part of the app it was and how many players were involved — no report contents and no names. Nothing about it is transmitted, and it is not included in backup files you make or in support packages (your device's own backups do copy it); it exists so a problem can be identified from the device it happened on.

If you ask us for help, we may ask you for a support package. The app makes one only when you choose Generate Support Package on the subscription screen, and it reaches us only if you send it. On a Mac it is saved to your Downloads folder; on an iPad, to Files › On My iPad › MatchTrack › Support. It is a ZIP of four text files:

  • the app's version, and on a Mac the macOS version and build, processor type and Mac model, or on an iPad the iPadOS version and iPad model;
  • your subscription status: whether it is active, the plan, the expiry date and whether it is in a grace period, and when the app last saved that;
  • how many workspaces you have, which one is open, and each one's name, season and team name;
  • on an iPad, up to 2,000 lines of the system log: the app's own lines and any error the system reported inside it, from the last 24 hours but no earlier than when the app last started. macOS does not let an App Store app read the system log, so on a Mac this file holds only a note that no log lines were found.

It does not contain your reports, your GPS files or your player photographs. The files are plain text, so you can read them before you send them.

Device information

On a Mac, the app reads no hardware identifier. Test versions before 2.0 saved the Mac's hardware identifier in the app's own preferences, to notice a move to a different Mac; this version deletes it the first time it opens. Your subscription is not tied to a machine — it follows your Apple Account.

The Mac app also asks macOS for the computer's name, and writes it into any backup file you make, along with the location of the app's data folder, which includes your macOS account name. Only when you make a support package does it read the macOS version, the processor type and the Mac model.

On an iPad, the app reads no hardware identifier. It reads the iPad's name — which iPadOS normally gives apps simply as "iPad" — its iPadOS version and its model, such as iPad16,5. The name goes into any backup file you make, and the version and model only into support packages.

Children

MatchTrack is a tool for coaches and performance staff, and is not directed at children. It collects no information about players or coaches. Player details entered or imported by a coach stay on the Mac or iPad they were entered on, and in that device's own backups, unless the coach exports or shares them.

Deleting your data

On a Mac, everything the app keeps is in its folder in your Library, ~/Library/Containers/com.thomasgrech.matchtrack; quit the app and delete that folder to remove it. On an iPad, deleting the app removes its data from the iPad.

Files you exported, backup files, support packages and your device's own backups are separate copies, and are yours to delete. On an iPad, though, anything left in Files › On My iPad › MatchTrack is deleted along with the app, so move a backup file you want to keep somewhere else first.

Deleting a workspace inside the app deletes its files, but its automatic restore points — and, on a Mac, copies the app's interface kept in earlier sessions — can stay in the app's data until that data is removed as above.

The only things held elsewhere are the subscription notices described above, which carry no name or email address, and any email you have sent us. If you have written to us, you can ask us to delete that correspondence.

Changes

If this policy ever changes, the updated version will be posted at this address with a new date at the top. Since the app collects so little, any change is likely to be clarification rather than substance.

If you contact us

If you email us for help, we receive your email address and whatever you attach — which may be a CSV with your players' names and performance figures, a backup file or a support package. Please send only what we ask for. It is kept in our support mailbox, which Google's Gmail hosts, only for as long as we need it to help you, and we delete it if you ask.
