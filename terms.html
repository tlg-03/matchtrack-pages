5. How MatchTrack counts devices

5.1 While you use MatchTrack with an active subscription, about once a day, it sends our service three things: Apple's signed record of the subscription; a random number made by that copy of the app; and whether the device is a Mac or an iPad. The random number is not taken from your device's hardware and carries no name or email address. Each user account on a Mac makes its own number, and so does each new installation on an iPad, so each usually counts as a separate device (a device set up from another's backup may share its number). Like any internet request, the check also shows your device's IP address to Cloudflare, which runs the service for us; we do not record it. We can connect any of this to you only if you write to us about your subscription. A seat assigned to you through Apple does not check in, because Apple gives each seat to one person.

5.2 We use this only to show the reminder in clause 5.3 and to help you if you write to us. We do not use it to decide whether anyone has breached this agreement, or to end a licence.

5.3 If more Macs, or more iPads, check in under one subscription within 30 days than the number we have set as usual for one person, the app shows a reminder of clause 4. A device you have replaced still counts for 30 days after it last checked in. A reminder is not a finding that you have breached this agreement: if all the devices are yours, or are used under clause 4.7, you can ignore it.

5.4 The check never locks MatchTrack or limits it, and MatchTrack works the same if the check cannot be made. Nothing in clause 4 changes how MatchTrack works: it works on every device signed in with the Apple Account that holds the subscription. Using several devices of your own is not a breach of this agreement; what matters is how many people use a subscription.
